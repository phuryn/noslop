To reseal a leaky tent seam, pitch the tent somewhere dry and well ventilated, then clean the seam and let it dry completely. Gently remove any loose, peeling seam sealer. Before you apply Gear Aid Seam Grip, check that your formula is compatible with your tent's fabric. Then spread a thin, even layer along the stitching, covering the needle holes and a little fabric on either side. Keep the temperature at or above 50°F, and let the sealant cure for 24 hours before you pack the tent or expose it to rain. Once it's cured, spray the seam with water and check inside for leaks.
