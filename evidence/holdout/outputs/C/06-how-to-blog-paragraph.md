Got a leaky tent seam? It's an easy fix. Set the tent up somewhere dry with good airflow, then wipe the seam clean and let it dry all the way. If the old sealer is peeling or loose, gently pick it off. Then brush a thin, even coat of Gear Aid Seam Grip along the stitching, making sure it covers the needle holes and spills a little onto the fabric on both sides. Before you start, double-check that your Seam Grip formula works with your tent's fabric. Try to work when it's at least 50°F out, and give the sealant a full 24 hours to cure before you pack the tent up or let it get rained on. When it's cured, spray the seam with water and look inside for any drips.
