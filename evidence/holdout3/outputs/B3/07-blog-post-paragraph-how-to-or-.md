I've been translating Spanish-English legal documents for twelve years, and every few months an agency offers me €0.04 a word to "post-edit" a machine-translated contract. They present it as a discount on the real work. It's a different and worse job, with the same liability and less of the thinking.

In a contract, one misplaced modal verb can shift an obligation from one party to the other. So can a false friend like *disposición* rendered as "disposition" instead of "provision," or a quietly dropped negation. Machine output is fluent enough to hide those errors, and that is what makes it dangerous. I can't skim a draft that reads smoothly. I have to check every clause against the source anyway, and I also have to untangle the machine's confident wrong guesses about terminology, jurisdiction, and legal concepts that have no clean equivalent between the two systems. So I read everything twice, once for what the source says and once for where the machine betrayed it, and then I rewrite whatever I can't trust. After all that I've saved no effort, and I've been paid a third of my rate to carry the same professional responsibility.

The client sees a saving on the invoice and assumes the risk shrank with it. It didn't. It moved to a cheaper, more rushed, less visible place, and in a contract dispute that's usually where the bill finally shows up.
