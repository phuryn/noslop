I've translated Spanish-English legal documents for twelve years. Every few months an agency offers me €0.04 a word to "post-edit" a machine-translated contract, as if that were a discount on the real work and not a different, worse job. The liability is the same, and I get less of the thinking. In a contract, one misplaced modal verb can shift an obligation from one party to the other. So can a false friend like *disposición* coming out as "disposition" instead of "provision," or a negation that quietly drops out. Machine output is fluent enough to hide those errors. I can't skim a draft that reads smoothly, so I verify every clause against the source anyway, and I also have to untangle the machine's confident wrong guesses about terminology, jurisdiction, and legal concepts with no clean equivalent between the two systems. That means reading everything twice, once to understand the source and once to catch where the machine betrayed it, and then rewriting whatever I can't trust. When I'm done I haven't saved any effort, and I've been paid a third of my rate to carry the same professional responsibility. The client sees a saving on the invoice and assumes the risk shrank with it. It didn't. It moved somewhere cheaper, more rushed, and less visible, and in a contract dispute that's usually where the bill finally shows up.
