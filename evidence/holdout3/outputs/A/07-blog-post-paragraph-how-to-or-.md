I've been translating Spanish-English legal documents for twelve years, and every few months an agency offers me €0.04 a word to "post-edit" a machine-translated contract. They present it as a discount on the real work. In practice it's a different job, and a worse one, with the same liability and less of the thinking. In a contract, one misplaced modal verb, one false friend like *disposición* rendered as "disposition" instead of "provision," or one quietly dropped negation can shift an obligation from one party to the other. Machine output is fluent enough to hide those errors, and that is what makes it dangerous. I can't skim a draft that reads smoothly. I have to check every clause against the source anyway, and I have to untangle the machine's confident wrong guesses about terminology, jurisdiction, and legal concepts that have no clean equivalent between the two systems. That means reading everything twice, once to understand the source and once to catch where the machine got it wrong, and then rewriting whatever I can't trust. By then I've saved no effort, and I've been paid a third of my rate to carry the same professional responsibility. The client sees a saving on the invoice and assumes the risk shrank with it. The risk just moved to a cheaper, more rushed, less visible place, and in a contract dispute that's usually where the bill finally shows up.
