It’s seven in the morning, a Tuesday and I’m standing in the shallow end of a swimming pool at the Ladywell Arena. My arms are folded tightly across my chest as though that way no-one can see just how scared I am. But they can. Joan can. She’s been a swimming instructor for hundreds of people but treats my fear with kindness and patience. She never tells me to relax, just to breathe out when my head is under the water, let go and let my feet fall away from the bottom of the pool. And the pool does take some of my weight. For the first few weeks I am as bad at learning to swim as I’ve been bad at anything in my life, and that’s saying a lot for a fifty two year old woman. But by the end I’m not so bothered. It’s humbling and at the same time liberating, this weekly few hours a week where I can be a beginner again. And when I first make it across the pool without touching the bottom I’m not triumphant, just quietly astonished.
