It was a wet Tuesday in November, about four months in. A woman in her seventies had been locked out of her terraced house off Bishopthorpe Road since the morning, and when I got there she was on the doorstep in the drizzle with a carrier bag of shopping, her cardigan pulled tight. I knelt at the door and worked the lock for maybe ten minutes, until I felt the last pin set. When it turned, she let out a small breath, like she'd been holding it all day. Then she pressed a flask of tea into my hands and apologised for the trouble.

I realised I hadn't thought about the screens, the P&L or the 7 a.m. dread I'd carried around London for years. Nobody in the City ever thanked me like that, and the money I made there never felt as plainly earned as that tea. I drove home with cold knees and a damp collar. For the first time in a long while, I didn't feel like I was waiting for my life to start.
