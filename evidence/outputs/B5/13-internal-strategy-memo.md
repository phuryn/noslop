Re: Recommendation to close our three mall stores at lease end and reinvest in Denver and online

Recommendation

I recommend we let the leases on our three mall stores expire in June 2027 without renewing them, and move the capital and operating dollars they use to the Denver flagship and our online business.

Why now

All three leases end in June 2027, so we can leave without early-termination penalties. Renewal decisions and landlord negotiations typically start 9 to 12 months before expiration, which means we need to decide in the next few months. If we wait, we could get pulled into renewal terms by default.

Rationale

1. Mall traffic is declining for good reasons we can't reverse. The stores depend on foot traffic we don't control, and it has been eroding. How they perform has more to do with anchor tenants and the health of each mall than with our own brand pull.
2. Our brand performs best at the flagship and online. Denver gives customers the full brand experience, and online reaches them wherever they are. Both offer stronger potential returns on extra investment than the mall stores do.
3. The mall stores take a lot of capital and labor for what they return. Rent, CAM charges, staffing, and buildout upkeep across three locations spread management attention and cash thin. Consolidating lets us put our talent and inventory in one place.
4. Closing at lease end keeps us from locking into another multi-year commitment in a channel we're already questioning.

Proposed reinvestment

- Denver flagship: more events and programming, a better in-store experience, and added staff to take on customers from the closing stores.
- Online: work on site performance, fulfillment speed, marketing, and customer retention, with a focus on converting mall-store customers.
- Customer transition: outreach to mall-store customers before we close, including promotions, local-pickup or shipping incentives, and an invitation to Denver.

Risks and how we'd handle them

Some mall-store revenue won't follow us, and some customers won't transfer. We'd start capturing customer data and reaching out at least six months before closing, and we'd track retention by store.

Employees will be affected. We'd tell them early, move people into Denver and online roles where we can, and offer severance and transition support to the others.

Relying on one physical location and one digital channel raises our exposure. We'd set clear performance thresholds and look again at pop-up or wholesale options if we need to.

Inventory and closing costs are another risk. We'd plan a phased wind-down and liquidation starting in Q1 2027.
