## Problem

When a caregiver calls in sick, their visits sit uncovered until a coordinator notices, finds someone to fill in, and fixes the schedule by hand. That usually means phone calls and texts: who's free, who's qualified, who lives close enough, who the client will actually accept. Then comes confirming with both the caregiver and the client. It all takes time, and call-outs tend to land early in the morning, when several visits are at risk at once.

The effects add up:

- **Clients go without care or wait for it.** A late or missed visit matters a lot to someone relying on help with medication, meals, mobility, or personal care.
- **Coordinators get stuck in reactive mode.** Rescheduling by hand pulls them away from planning, onboarding, and talking with clients. It's hardest to do well when call-outs bunch up.
- **Results depend on who's on shift.** Speed and quality change with the coordinator, the time of day, and how many call-outs come in.
- **Agencies pay for it.** Missed visits hurt client satisfaction, retention, and compliance, and last-minute scrambling often means overtime or poorly matched assignments.

Right now, nothing detects a call-out and acts on it automatically. At Sunrise Home Care, our pilot partner, coordinators handle each case one by one.

## Goals

**Primary goals**

1. **Reschedule affected visits within 30 minutes of a reported call-out.** A visit counts as rescheduled once a replacement caregiver has confirmed, or once it has been passed to a coordinator with a clear reason.
2. **Cut the manual work coordinators do on call-outs.** They should step in only for exceptions, not routine reassignments.

**Success metrics** (we'll measure baselines with Sunrise before the pilot starts and confirm targets then)

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Share of affected visits rescheduled within 30 minutes | To be set from baseline (proposed: 80%+) |
| Median time from call-out to confirmed replacement | Under 30 minutes |
| Coordinator time spent per call-out | Lower than baseline (proposed: 50%+ reduction) |
| Share of call-outs resolved without coordinator action | To be set from baseline |
| Visits left uncovered | No increase vs. baseline |

**Secondary goals**

- Keep match quality up: replacements should meet requirements for qualification, availability, location, and client preference.
- Keep clients and caregivers in the loop automatically, so nobody has to chase for updates.
- Let coordinators see what the system did and why, with an easy way to override it.

**Pilot scope**

The pilot runs with Sunrise Home Care in January. We'll use it to set baselines, test whether the 30-minute target is realistic, and measure how much coordinator effort drops. That will inform the decision on a broader rollout.

**Non-goals**

- Replacing coordinators or taking away their ability to override decisions.
- Long-term schedule optimization or call-out forecasting.
- Handling planned absences like vacations or scheduled leave.
