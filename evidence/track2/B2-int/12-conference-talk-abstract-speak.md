Finding Leaks with Pressure Data We Already Had

Abstract

Mesa Valley Water Authority found leaks using pressure data from its existing SCADA sensors, with no new hardware. Over 18 months, non-revenue water dropped from 14% to 9%, which saves about 410 million gallons a year. We can't say how much of that drop came from leaks found this way and how much from other work.

One find was a major main leak under an elementary school, which field tech Carlos Ruiz traced using the model's alerts. The early alerts were rough: about 30% were false positives in the first months. I also don't think the machine learning was essential. Simple threshold rules got us most of the gains, and the whole project cost $85,000. This talk covers what we did and why I'd tell other utilities to start simple with the data they already have.

Short Bio

Dr. Naomi Fischer is a Senior Water Systems Engineer at Mesa Valley Water Authority, with 12 years at the authority and a PhD from Arizona State.
