## Problem

When a caregiver calls in sick, their visits go uncovered until a coordinator notices, finds a replacement, and updates the schedule by hand. Coordinators typically do this by phone and text. They check who is available, who is qualified and close enough, and who the client will accept, then confirm with both the caregiver and the client. It takes time, and call-outs often come early in the morning, when several visits are at risk at once.

- Clients go without care or wait for it. Late or missed visits hit clients who depend on help with medication, meals, mobility, or personal care.
- Coordinators lose time to reactive work. Manual rescheduling pulls them away from planning, onboarding, and client communication, and it's hardest to do well when call-outs cluster.
- Outcomes depend on who is working. Speed and quality vary by coordinator, time of day, and call-out volume.
- Agencies bear the cost. Missed visits put client satisfaction, retention, and compliance at risk, and last-minute scrambling often leads to overtime or poorly matched assignments.

No system currently detects a call-out and acts on it automatically. Sunrise Home Care, our pilot partner, relies on coordinators to handle each case individually.

## Goals

### Primary goals

1. Reschedule affected visits within 30 minutes of a call-out being reported. Rescheduling is complete when a replacement caregiver has confirmed, or when the visit has been escalated to a coordinator with a clear reason.
2. Reduce the manual work coordinators do on call-outs. Coordinators should step in only for exceptions, not routine reassignments.

### Success metrics

We'll measure baselines with Sunrise before the pilot begins, and the targets are still to be confirmed.

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Share of affected visits rescheduled within 30 minutes | To be set from baseline (proposed: 80%+) |
| Median time from call-out to confirmed replacement | Under 30 minutes |
| Coordinator time spent per call-out | Reduced vs. baseline (proposed: 50%+) |
| Share of call-outs resolved without coordinator action | To be set from baseline |
| Visits left uncovered | No increase vs. baseline |

### Secondary goals

- Keep match quality up: replacements meet qualification, availability, location, and client-preference requirements.
- Keep clients and caregivers informed automatically, so no one has to chase status.
- Show coordinators what the system did and why, and make it easy for them to override.

### Pilot scope

The pilot runs with Sunrise Home Care in January. It will establish baselines, validate the 30-minute target, and measure the reduction in coordinator effort. The results will inform the decision on broader rollout.

### Non-goals

- Replacing coordinators or removing their ability to override decisions.
- Long-term schedule optimization or forecasting of call-outs.
- Handling planned absences such as vacations or scheduled leave.
