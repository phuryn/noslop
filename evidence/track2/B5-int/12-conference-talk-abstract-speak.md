Finding Leaks with Pressure Data We Already Had

Abstract

Over 18 months, Mesa Valley Water Authority took non-revenue water from 14% to 9%, a drop that saves about 410 million gallons a year. We did it with our existing SCADA pressure sensors, and the whole project cost about $85,000. Carlos Ruiz, one of our field techs, used the model's alerts to trace a major main leak under an elementary school.

It wasn't clean at first. In the first few months about 30% of the leak alerts were false positives. I also don't think the machine learning was essential. I think simple threshold rules got us most of the gains.

You'll leave knowing how to get real results out of the pressure data you already have, without buying hardware.

Short Bio

Dr. Naomi Fischer is a Senior Water Systems Engineer at Mesa Valley Water Authority. She has been there for 12 years and holds a PhD from Arizona State. The project in this talk helped take the utility's non-revenue water from 14% to 9%.
