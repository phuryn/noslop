Finding Leaks with Pressure Data We Already Had

Abstract

Mesa Valley Water Authority found leaks using the SCADA pressure sensors it already had, so it bought no new hardware. We ran the pressure data through a model that sent alerts when something looked off. The first big catch was a major main leak under an elementary school, which field tech Carlos Ruiz traced using those alerts.

Over 18 months, non-revenue water fell from 14% to 9%, saving about 410 million gallons a year. The whole project cost $85,000. We can't say how much of that drop came from the pressure analysis alone.

In the first few months, about 30% of the alerts were false positives, which was a pain. I also don't think the machine learning was essential. Simple threshold rules got us most of the gains, and I'd tell other utilities to start there.

Short Bio

Dr. Naomi Fischer is a senior engineer at Mesa Valley Water Authority and has been there 12 years. She has a PhD from Arizona State.
